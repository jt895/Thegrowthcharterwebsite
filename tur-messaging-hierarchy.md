# Part 1: The Messaging Hierarchy

## 1.1 The foundation

**The emotional problem we solve**

Our clients are accountable for growth decisions they can't be certain about. They respond the way most capable people do: with activity. More campaigns, more content, more spend. Activity feels like progress, but it doesn't answer the question that keeps them up at night: am I backing the right things?

**The tangible problem underneath it**

Money and effort spread across unranked activity. No agreed evidence base for deciding what matters. Budget committed before the real issue is understood.

**The enemy**

Activity without certainty. We never position against competitors, agencies or the client's past decisions. We position against the state of guessing while spending.

**The brand promise**

We replace doubt with evidence, ranked priorities and a plan you can defend and own. Growth follows, but the certainty comes first.

---

## 1.2 Positioning statement

For leaders accountable for growth, whether they own the business or answer for the budget, The United Republic is the strategic partner that finds where growth is really available, ranks what matters most by value, and builds the capability to act. Unlike agencies that sell activity, we sell certainty: evidence-based choices you can defend to anyone, including yourself.

## 1.3 Core message and pillars

**Core message (the roof):**

> Growth needs sharper choices, not more activity.

This line survives from the current site because it's the right line. It names the enemy (activity) and the cure (choices) in nine words. Everything below supports it.

**Pillar 1: Know where growth really is**

- Message: Most businesses aren't short on effort. They're short on certainty about where the effort should go. We find where growth is actually available before a dollar moves.
- Emotional payoff: Relief. The end of guessing.
- Tangible payoff: Spend stops leaking into activity that was never going to pay.

**Pillar 2: Decide what matters most, and be able to say why**

- Message: Opportunities ranked by value, not by noise or habit. Every choice comes with its evidence attached, so the decision holds up in any room it's questioned in.
- Emotional payoff: Defensibility. Walking into the board meeting, the ministerial briefing or your own Monday morning already knowing you can answer the hard question.
- Tangible payoff: Faster decisions, because the criteria are agreed. Budget goes where the return is.
- Proof: The advisory process. Commercial-first lens: customers, brand, sales, margins and capacity assessed together.

**Pillar 3: Own the plan, and the capability to run it**

- Message: You don't rent our thinking. We build it into your business, then make ourselves unnecessary. The confidence you gain is yours to keep.
- Emotional payoff: Restored self-respect. The client ends the engagement more capable, not more dependent.
- Tangible payoff: No ongoing retainer dependency. Internal capability that compounds.
- Proof: "Capability, not dependency" is already the operating model. Case studies showing clients running plans themselves.

## 1.4 Audience layers

The wound is the same for both audiences: sole accountability without sufficient certainty. The texture differs, so the emphasis shifts.

**Founders and owners (The Growth Program)**

- Their version of the problem: isolation. No board, no CMO, nobody senior to check their thinking against. The money at risk is personal.
- Lead with: Pillar 1 (know where growth really is), then Pillar 3 (you own it).
- Name the feeling directly. This audience rewards recognition: "busy, spending, still not sure it's working" describes their Tuesday.
- Words that work: sure, certain, backing, own, next step, evidence, worth it.
- Words to avoid: transformation, journey, unlock, empower, supercharge, passion.

**Government, corporate and complex organisations (Strategic Advisory)**

- Their version of the problem: exposure. They must defend the spend to a minister, a board or an audit. They need to genuinely believe the decision was sound and be able to show their working.
- Lead with: Pillar 2 (defensible decisions), supported by independence.
- Keep the emotion implicit. This audience buys reassurance but needs to look rational doing it. The site should read as rigour; the reassurance is what rigour feels like.
- Words that work: independent, evidence, defensible, before committing budget, clear answer, counsel.
- Words to avoid: anything that sounds like an agency pitching for the downstream work.

## 1.6 Tone of voice rules

1. Plain sentences. If a line couldn't be said out loud to a client across a table, cut it.
2. Confidence through specificity, not adjectives. "Ranked by value" beats "powerful insights."
3. Never sell. State, offer, invite. The 20-minute call framing ("no pitch") is the tonal benchmark for everything.
4. Name the client's reality before naming the solution. Recognition earns the right to be heard.
5. Short claims, visible proof. Every assertion should sit within a scroll of its evidence.

### The central idea

Every business runs on assumptions. Why customers buy. Which work is most valuable. Where growth will come from. Some of them are right. Some are about the business, not the customer. Some are old wives' tales that have never been checked.

### How it works

## Five stages. One plan. Evidence at every step.

Each stage produces the evidence and decisions the next one needs. We don't jump into action before we understand your business, your objectives and what success looks like, because action without direction is how the money got wasted last time.

**01 Discovery: The Business Now**
How you and your team see the business today: goals, customers, business model, capacity, risks, and where you personally want to end up. That last one matters more than most consultants admit.

**02 Evidence and analysis: The Opportunity**
We test the assumptions against customer research, real numbers and market reality. Some will hold up. Some won't. Either way, you'll know instead of wondering. Then we put a value on each opportunity and rank them by what they're worth.

**03 A clearer place in the market: Branding and Positioning**
What you're really selling, the problem you solve, why the right customers should choose you, and how you stand apart in a way you can actually deliver on.

**04 Who matters and how to reach them: Audiences, Messaging and Channels**
Which customers matter most, who decides and who influences, what to say to each of them, and the best way to reach them. No spray, no pray.

**05 Turn the plan into action: Go To Market and Implementation**
A staged plan: who does what, when, what it costs, and how you'll know it's working. Plus the tools your team needs to run it without us.

# Part 4: Implementation notes

1. **The homepage hero stays rational, the subhead does the emotional work.** "Certainty about where the effort should go" is the doubt named in a register the government audience can also sit with. Don't push the homepage further than that.

2. **The Growth Program page gets the one-two hero.** Recognition ("You're busy. You're spending. And you're still not sure it's working.") followed immediately by the existing promise line ("the growth you're looking for is already in your business"). The current page opens with the promise alone, which is warm but skips the moment of being seen. If the pain-led H1 tests too confronting, the fallback is keeping the current H1 and moving the recognition line to open the Assumptions section.

3. **Promote the Viability Session.** It's currently fine print under the pricing table, and it's the single best answer to the emotional problem on the whole site: $450, credited in full, and an honest verdict on whether the Program will pay for itself. That's certainty sold in miniature. Give it its own visual block.

4. **Two typos to fix on the live page regardless of anything else:** "sizes it's value" should be "sizes its value" in the hero, and "Some of them right" in the Assumptions section is missing "are".

5. **"Growth is the receipt" is a keepable brand line.** It compresses the whole strategy: certainty is the product, growth is the evidence. Consider it for proposals, decks and the About page.

6. **Strategic Advisory copy is deliberately not rewritten here.** The current framing is close to right for that audience. The one addition worth carrying over when you touch that page: "need the decision to hold up before the money moves", used in the homepage pathway copy.

7. **Sequence for rollout:** live-page typo fixes immediately, homepage subhead and closing CTA next (low risk, immediate sharpening), then the Growth Program page as a full replacement, then Advisory refinements.
